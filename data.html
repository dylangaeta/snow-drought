Anomaly heatmaps by product and time

Standardized anomaly (σ) per product: one row per product/response, one column per month or year. Each row is sign-flipped to its own variable's stress direction (e.g. low snowpack is the stress direction, not high), so brown always means more stress and teal always means less stress, regardless of the variable's raw sign convention.

Every row is standardized against that product's own baseline (the month's or season's anomaly divided by the standard deviation of that same month or season across the product's baseline years), so a given σ is the same number of standard deviations from normal in each product's own climatology — comparable across rows in that sense. But baselines differ in period and length: short-record products (e.g. SMAP, GRACE) reference a shorter climate epoch and estimate that standard deviation from fewer years, so read a row's color as its standing within its own history, not as a strict cross-product ranking.

Regional summary vs. fixed calendar-month climatology

Computed on demand from the same mean-centered anomaly values as Time Series. Color = physical stress direction, not raw sign: more stress (drier/hotter/less productive) vs. less stress. Native indices (SPI/SPEI/EDDI/PDSI/ForDRI/ESI) are already a fixed-timescale statistic (e.g. SPI-03 = trailing 3 months), so a season window shows the reading "as of <month>" instead of re-aggregating it. Percent of mean is shown only for physical amounts (not temperature-like quantities, indices, net carbon exchange, anomaly products, fire or drought-category extent) and only where the year-to-year spread over the baseline is no larger than the baseline mean.
